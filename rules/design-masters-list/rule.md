Have you ever had a simple UI improvement ready to go, but it sat in limbo for days waiting on design approval? Or worse, it got rushed through by a dev without review, leading to inconsistent UX or accruing design debt? That’s the problem the Design Masters list solves.

<!--endintro-->

When your team doesn’t know who has the authority to approve UI/UX changes, small visual tweaks can turn into unnecessary blockers. Worse, in the name of progress, developers might skip the process entirely, leading to long-term inconsistencies. Instead, create a “Design Masters” list to formalize a pecking order of who is trusted to approve design decisions.

## What is a Design Masters list?

The Design Masters list is a documented list of people who are allowed to approve design changes. It typically includes:

1. **UI/UX Designers** – Always the first preference
2. **Graphic Designers** - Ideal for aesthetic changes
3. **Trusted Developers** – For small, non-controversial changes

## Benefits of a Design Masters list

* Prevents blockers on small UI changes
* Maintains design consistency
* Reduces unnecessary design debt
* Empowers developers with a clear process

## How to implement it

### 1. Define the scope of approval

Clarify what kind of changes a dev on the Design Masters list can approve. E.g.:

* ✅ Spacing, alignment, padding
* ✅ Minor copy tweaks (if UX writing rules are followed)
* ✅ Color adjustments using the approved palette
* ❌ New component designs
* ❌ Major layout overhauls

### 2. Ensure minimum design literacy

To keep quality high, don’t just add any dev to the list. Require each dev to:

* Watch a small set of internal videos on design basics
* Complete a short design crash course (e.g. “Design for Developers” on Frontend Masters or similar)
* Pair-review changes with a designer before they start approving solo

### 3. Publish and share the list

Add the list to your team wiki or documentation. It should be clear who is currently on the list and when it was last reviewed.

## Tip: Encourage fallback, not bypass

Devs on the list should always try to get a designer to review first. This is not a shortcut – it's a fallback when blocked.

## Tip: Review periodically

Every 3-6 months, review the Design Masters list:

* Are the right people on it?
* Have any lost design context?
* Are there new devs who should be added after training?

By maintaining a Design Masters list, you prevent bottlenecks and reduce poor UX decisions while still respecting the importance of consistent design.
